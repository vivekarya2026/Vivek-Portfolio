Background

I started working at Programming Pathshala in March 2021 as their in-house Product Designer. I worked directly with the Head of Product to give their platform a total makeover.

‍

Programming Pathshala is a learning platform that teaches people all about data structures & algorithms and full-stack development. It's perfect for students and professionals looking to boost their skills. They also team up with businesses and government organizations to help their employees switch careers. Since they were getting ready for a big investment, they needed urgent help with design – they wanted a new look and a better user experience.

‍

One of my main jobs was creating and managing a brand new design system.

‍

‍

DETAILS

‍

Scope and considerations

As the only designer, I had to be smart about building our design system while juggling other UX projects. It needed to grow quickly, so I worked closely with the product and engineering teams to prioritize what to build first. We focused on reusable parts and flexible templates to get our product out the door fast, without sacrificing a solid design foundation.

‍

‍

Not just a set of components

I teamed up with the Head of Product to make sure user research was a core part of our Design System. We ran usability studies on existing app, which really helped shape the components and interactions we built.

Something else I focused on was designing with real data. While I love lorem ipsum for early wireframes, I always swap in real data as things get more final. This helps me catch tricky issues early on, like what happens if a title is too long, or an image is missing. It's all about getting ahead of those "what ifs" and building a system that works in the real world.

‍

‍

Laying the foundations

Early on, I focused on picking the right look and feel – fonts, colors, the whole vibe. I showed a few ideas to the team, trying to balance a modern feel with something that wouldn't overwhelm users. It's a tough time, especially after a layoff, so I kept that in mind for everything from colors to the little bits of text.

‍

The goal was more supportive coach, less added stress. Once that was set, I built out page templates, playing around with how to organize everything and make it easy to navigate. I worked closely with the engineers to make sure it'd look good on different screen sizes.

‍

‍

We started building a ton of components using 'Atomic Design' as our guide. Those components were always evolving, so we worked closely with the devs to keep everything aligned between our Design System. Super important for a smooth workflow!

‍

‍

‍

Accessibility baked into the process

Starting a Design System from the ground up meant baking accessibility right in. We focused on:

  • Colors that pop: Making sure they're easy to see on any background, even when buttons are doing their thing.
  • Fonts you can actually read: Especially on mobile, where everything's smaller. Oh, and buttons that are big enough to tap easily.
  • No more guessing games: Using text labels with those cute little icons on mobile menus, and keeping everything consistent across the board.

We've made great strides, but there's always room to grow, especially with tight timelines and tough choices. This sets a solid foundation and makes accessibility a team effort.

‍

‍

Conculsion

Within 3 months of joining, I led the tool's redesign, launching it on schedule. The Design System was instrumental:

  • Visual Consistency: It created a unified look for the rebrand.
  • Clear Decision-Making: It provided a framework for navigating conflicting team requests.
  • Efficient Development: It sped up the process with reusable components and a single reference point.
